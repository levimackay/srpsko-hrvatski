## General Accent Rules

- *A* - Stressed syllable
- "Á" - Rising accent (When a word ends with a vowel it is basically guaranteed to have a Rising accent because it is cut short, this is not documented.)
- "À" - Falling accent
- The second to last syllable most commonly has the stress for nouns.
- The third to last syllable most commonly has the stress for verbs.
- Never accent on the last syllable when a word is in Nominative/Infinitive Form, if you cannot tell which syllable has the marking, guess that it is the second or third to last syllable.
- If a second to last syllable is an "i" (like "admini*STRA*cija") the third to last syllable probably has the stress. If a word ends in -(x)ija, -nica, or -nik the syllable just before probably has the stress. ("(x)" being a placemarker for any consonant)
- Words ending in -stvo, -štvo will always have (+a).
- Nouns ending in -nik will always have a (k->c) consonant change.
- Nouns ending in -tak will have a (a) and (+a).
- The general rule regarding stress is that it can fall on any syllable except the last, although there are exceptions to the rule, e.g. maturant (a high school graduate), where the stress falls on the final syllable. In a word of two syllables, the stress will fall on the first syllable:
  - kola
  - pivo
- In a three-syllable word, it will fall on either the first or the second syllable:
  - sudija
  - sladoled

## Arebica

Arebica in Bosnia refers to Arabic script, which was used to write Bosnian before the Latin alphabet. It's a cool part of our history, showing the influence of the Ottoman Empire. You might spot it in old documents or museum exhibits, but it's not something we use every day anymore. Some missionaries learn this for fun. I thought it was fun

## Vocative Case for Foreign Names

Foreign names typically do not have vocative forms in Serbian grammar. The vocative case is primarily used for Serbian names and nouns. However, if a foreign name has been integrated into Serbian usage for an extended period, it may occasionally be adapted into the vocative case according to Serbian grammar rules, although such instances are rare.

## Infinitives

This varies in-between different countries, but this is equivalent to things in english like "to eat" "to sleep" etc. In Croatian these can be used after any verb that is conjugated in the Present tense. In Serbian however, it is rarely used except in certain situations. One of these situations takes place after what is called a "modal verb." These are verbs that wouldn't really make sense by themselves or couldn't stand by themselves without another verb (can, may, might, should, need to, have to) Using the infinitive isn't mandatory in these situations, but it is common to see.
