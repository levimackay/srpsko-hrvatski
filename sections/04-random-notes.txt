General Accent Rules:
●  *A* - Stressed syllable
●  "Á" - Rising accent (When a
word ends with a vowel it is
basically guaranteed to have
a Rising accent because it is
cut short, this is not
documented.)
●  "À" - Falling accent
●  The second to last syllable
most commonly has the stress
for nouns.
●  The third to last syllable
most commonly has the stress
for verbs.
●  Never accent on the last
syllable when a word is in
Nominative/Infinitive Form,
if you cannot tell which
syllable has the marking,
guess that it is the second
or third to last syllable.
●  If a second to last syllable
is an "i" (like
"admini*STRA*cija'') the
third to last syllable
probably has the stress. If a
word ends in -(x)ija, -nica,
or -nik the syllable just
before probably has the
stress. ("(x)" being a
placemarker for any
consonant)
●  Words ending in -stvo, -štvo
will always have (+a).
●  Nouns ending in -nik will
always have a (k->c)
consonant change.
●  Nouns ending in -tak will
have a (a) and (+a).
●  The general rule regarding
stress is that it can fall on
any syllable except the last,
although there are exceptions
to the rule, e.g.maturant (a
(a high school graduate),
where the stress falls on the
final syllable. In a word of
two syllables, the stress
will fall on the first
syllable:
○  kola
○  pivo
●  In a three-syllable word, it
will fall on either the first
or the second syllable:
○  sudija
○  sladoled

Vocative Case for Foreign Names in
Serbian Grammar:
●  Foreign names typically do
not have vocative forms in
Serbian grammar. The vocative
case is primarily used for
Serbian names and nouns.
However, if a foreign name
has been integrated into
Serbian usage for an extended
period, it may occasionally
be adapted into the vocative
case according to Serbian
grammar rules, although such
instances are rare.

Infinitives:
This varies in-between different
countries, but this is equivalent
to things in english like “to eat”
“to sleep” etc. In Croatian these
can be used after any verb that is
conjugated in the Present tense.
In Serbian however, it is rarely
used except in certain situations.
One of these situations takes
place after what is called a
“modal verb.” These are verbs that
wouldn't really make sense by
themselves or couldn’t stand by
themselves without another verb
(can, may, might, should, need to,
have to) Using the infinitive
isn't mandatory in these
situations, but it is common to
see.

Lang Tips/Accent
Accents are tricky and a hard
thing to get down. Unfortunately
there is no way to completely get
rid of your accent. You can make
it better and keep working at it,
but truly perfecting the accent
would take years. (Example: German
speakers speaking english. It is
perfect and the accent is great,
but there are still words from
time to time where an accent is
present. And they study english
for years.) Focusing on the
accent, or working on it, is
completely up to each and every
missionary. Some prioritize it
more than others. I prioritized it
because I didnt want to sound like
I was taking a high-school level
spanish class. (Hhhhhhola amigo.
***imagine the most white accent
ever haha***) Anyways, Here are
some of my notes from my mission.
It's not perfect and by all means,
make a copy of this and make
correcrions as needed, but these
are the things I have noticed.
●●●●●●
These are comments or rules that
specifically apply to accentation
of words and speech.
- Accent changes depending on the
case sometimes. (Especially in
genative with the reappearing a.
Watch out) Not super hard, just
takes practice
- Accent will also change
sometimes depending on whether or
not the verb is perfective or
Imperfective. (This relates more
to the length. It varies)
- Additionally, the accent changes
on some verbs depending on the
tense it is in (past,present,
future)
- In Serbian, stress usually falls
on the first syllable of the word.
If the word is longer (usually 3
or more syllables, then the stress
will be on the second to last or
the third to last syllable. THIS
