Navigating Formality: Using "Ti"
vs "Vi" in Conversations
When we’re close to someone, or
they’re about our age, we’ll
address them as ti (you). But when
we talk to older people or our
superiors, rules of conduct
dictate us to tell them Vi (you,
but formal) when speaking to them.
Although older people in Serbia
find it insulting when youth don’t
address them accordingly, don’t
worry. As soon as they hear
Serbian isn’t your native
language, you won’t have any
problem with them.
Both forms are important. I see
missionaries sometimes only use
one or the other. Using the wrong
one at the wrong time can be
offensive and really get under
someone’s skin. It’s like if I
walked up to President Cordray and
said “Yo what’s up bro ham” First
off, i would die because that’s
weird. Second, he would think that
is weird and maybe take offense to
it. Aka - learn both, don’t be
weird.
Expressing Gratitude: Choosing
Between "Za" and "Na"
Za + accusative is used when
expressing gratitude for something
specific or an action. It
indicates what you are thankful
for directly. For example:
-  "Zahvalan sam za pomoć." (I
am thankful for the help.)
-  "Hvala za poklon." (Thanks
for the gift.)
-  "Zahvaljujem za podršku." (I
thank you for the support.)
Na + dative is used to express
thanks directed towards someone
for their effort, intention, or
attribute. It implies gratitude
towards someone rather than for a
specific thing. Examples include:
"Hvala na razumevanju." (Thank you
for your understanding.)
"Hvala na trudu." (Thanks for the
effort.)
"Hvala na ljubaznosti." (Thank you
for your kindness.)

Imperfective Verbs (Neprefektivni
glagoli):
Usage:
Continuous or Ongoing Actions:
Imperfective verbs describe
actions that were ongoing,
habitual, or repeated in the past.
They focus on the process rather
than the completion of an action.
   - Example: "Kada sam bio mali,
često sam čitao knjige." (When I
was young, I used to read books
often.)
