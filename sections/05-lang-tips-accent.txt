- Diminutives (smaller versions of
things): When adding suffixes to a
word, the stress often remains on
the same syllable. (Example: kuća
→ kućica)
- Compound Words: Each component
retains its original stress.
(Example: čokolada + kolač =
čokoladakolač)
- Loanwords: Stress placement may
follow the original language's
rules or adapt to Serbian patterns
over time (Example: kompjúter,
šokoláda)
- In Serbian, stressed syllables
often contain long vowels, while
unstressed syllables usually have
short vowels. (People from Čačak
and southern Serbia "over stress"
their words and they drag out
their vowels. They make the stress
longer. This causes them to talk
slower and it takes more time to
talk because of it. It’s the
reason some people see the
accent/people from Čačak/southern
Serbia as stupid)
- The stress often remains on the
stem of a word. Certain endings,
such as the genitive plural "-a",
can affect stress placement (like
i mentioned above) (Example:
knjìga → knjígama. The accent goes
from a long to a short)
- In Serbian, the accent placement
in verbs changes when
transitioning from the present
tense to the imperative form (we
call this command form). In the
present tense, the stress is
usually on the root or the suffix
of the verb(aka the normal normal
spot). However, in the imperative
form, the stress shifts to the
ending of the verb. For example,
in the present tense verb
"razmišljam" (I am thinking), the
stress is on the root "miš,"
whereas in the imperative
"razmišljaj" (Think!), the stress
shifts to the ending "-aj." This
accent change is a key distinction
between these two verb forms

LATIN VS CYRILLIC
This is one of the biggest
differences between these and
often the one that missionaries
fear the most. Latin is what we
use in English. Normal normal.
Cyrillics is the one that my mom
says “looks like Russian.” But
guess what?!?!  In all honesty, it
just takes time and lots of
patience. НЕ БРИНИТЕ!
You will be slower at reading in
Cyrillics at first, but it will
become so much easier if you keep
working at it. I recommend a few
things that worked for me.
Write sentences and other useful
phrases you already know in
Cyrillics. (The 12 week
work-packet in the AP drive is a
great resource for this)
Read the Book of Mormon in Serbian
OUT-LOUD daily.
Put your flash cards (Quizlet or
physical) in cyrillics.
These things (which hopefully you
are already doing haha) are just
for frequency. As you see it more
and make that connection between
the latin alphabet and cyrillc, it
will become so much easier.
One last thing. All missionaries
should learn Serbian Italics.
(This is that funny font that all
the Scripture Chapter headings are
written in.) It is always awkward
when you are called on to read and
you can’t read the chapter heading
for Book of Mormon night. This
isn’t as important as the main
alphabet, but definitely worth
learning (so you don’t look like a
bozo)
